Module 01 · Capital Rules

Drawdown Mechanics & High-Water Mark Traps

The #1 reason prop-firm traders fail is failing to calculate the trailing drawdown floor relative to unrealized peak equity.

1.1 Trailing vs Static Drawdown

A static drawdown is a fixed distance below your starting balance. On a $100k account with a 10% max loss, the floor is a hard $90,000 — no matter how high your equity climbs first.

A trailing drawdown moves. The floor is a fixed percentage below the highest point your equity has reached, and it never comes back down. On a 5% trailing drawdown:

  • Start — floor sits at $95,000 (5% below $100k).
  • Equity peaks at $107,000 — the floor rises to $101,650 (5% below the peak).
  • Equity pulls back — the floor stays at $101,650. It only moves up, never down.
Plain English: your "allowed loss" is measured from your best point, not your start. Profits you haven't locked in still drag the floor up with you.

1.2 The High-Water Mark Trap

Once the floor locks at starting capital (most firms lock it at $100k as soon as you're in profit), the real danger zone is the first +0% to +5% of gains. A run-up followed by a normal retracement can breach the floor even when your balance is still above the static loss limit.

  • Lock profits early — trail your stop or take partials before a pullback.
  • Know your real floor — recalculate it against the peak, not the start.
  • Buffer before you scale — build a cushion so a normal drawdown can't breach.
The trap: a $98k balance can breach a $102k trailing floor — even though it is still above the $95k static floor. The firm's violation triggers off the trailing number.
Module 02 · Sizing Math

Risk Sizing & Phase Calibration

Fixed risk-per-trade with a phase-based curve keeps the math boring and the outcome reliable.

2.1 Capital Preservation Math

Risk a fixed, small percentage of the account per trade — never a dollar figure your emotions can inflate. The size is set by the stop distance (ATR-based), so a wide stop means fewer contracts, not more risk.

100k Challenge Account Rules

  • Account Size — $100,000
  • Max Overall Loss — $10,000 (10%)
  • Max Daily Loss — $5,000 (5%)
  • Risk per trade — $350 – $500 (0.35% – 0.5%)

Phase Sizing Curve

  • Phase 1 — 0.5% risk · +10% target
  • Phase 2 — 0.25% risk · +5% target
  • Funded — 0.25% until +3% buffer banked
0.5% risk = 20 consecutive losses to reach 10% max loss. 0.25% = 40. The curve makes a blowout a non-event — the challenge ends by violating rules, not by one bad trade.

2.2 Hard Circuit Breakers

These are non-negotiable kill-switches. They exist to stop a bad session from becoming a bad week — and a bad week from becoming a blown evaluation.

Trade & Session Limits

  • Stop trading after 2 consecutive losses in one day.
  • Max open positions — 1 trade at a time.
  • Cap daily risk at 1.0% — close the platform at the ceiling.

News & Hold Rules

  • Zero entries 5 minutes before/after high-impact news.
  • No overnight holds unless the firm explicitly permits them.
  • Never add to a loser — no averaging down, no revenge trades.
Circuit breakers are the difference between "in the challenge" and "looking for the next challenge fee."
Module 03 · Execution Roadmap

30-Day Evaluation Phase Progression

Two evaluation phases (Phase 1 +10%, Phase 2 +5%) run through three 10-day execution blocks.

3.1 Days 1–10 · Phase 1 Accumulation

The opening block is about building the habit architecture, not hitting the target on day three. Trade only London and NY AM kill zones. Accumulate with high-probability trend-continuation setups.

Phase 1 · Accumulate Days 1–10

Build the base

Risk 0.5% per trade toward the +10% Phase 1 target. Spread gains across the minimum required trading days — no single-day heroics.

Risk 0.50% / trade Target +10% Daily cap 1.0% Max 2 losses / day

3.2 Days 11–20 · Phase 2 Consolidation

De-risk. You're now defending Phase 1 gains while finishing the +5% Phase 2 target. Lower the size, raise the quality bar, and run the consistency check — no day above 35% of the target.

Phase 2 · Consolidate Days 11–20

Protect & comply

Risk 0.25%–0.35% per trade. Prefer reversals at session sweeps. Verify the consistency rule before the compliance check.

Risk 0.25–0.35% Target +5% Daily cap 0.75% Consistency < 35% / day

3.3 Days 21–30 · Funded Buffer & Scale

The final block transitions to the funded account. Build a +3% safety buffer before requesting the first payout, then scale only after +10% total growth. The challenge is won when the target is locked — not when day 30 arrives.

Funded · Buffer & Scale Days 21–30

Protect everything

Risk 0.25% base. Bank the buffer, request the payout on schedule, and scale only after the cushion is secure.

Risk 0.25% base Buffer +3–5% First payout 14 days / 80% Scale +10% growth
Module 04 · Execution

Confluence Alignment & Execution Checklist

Bar-close triggers only. Three-confluence alignment before risk. Circuit breakers after.

4.1 Three-Confluence Alignment

All three filters must agree on the closed bar before you risk capital:

1. Trend — Trend Cloud BULL or BEAR (never RANGE).
2. Structure — liquidity sweep + locked pivot / breaker context.
3. Value — FVG retest and/or Anchored VWAP discount / premium alignment.
If one of the three core filters disagrees, you wait. Precision over urgency.

4.2 Pre-Entry Execution Checklist

Run this before every order, in order:

HTF bias marked — H4/Daily direction identified and written down.
Kill zone active — London (02:00–05:00) or NY AM (08:30–11:00 EST).
Liquidity sweep confirmed — BSL above a high or SSL below a low, closed back inside.
Entry mapped — 3-candle FVG retest at 50% CE, or Breaker Block retest.
Risk sized — 0.5% (Phase 1) / 0.25% (Phase 2) with stop and target set.
News clear — no high-impact event within the next 5 minutes.
Breaker check — if 2 consecutive losses today, stand down.
Hard rule: never add to a losing position. No averaging down. No revenge trades. Stop out clean.
Playbooks · Two setups

Evaluation Execution Playbooks

Numbered rules in plain English. Same language you use at the desk.

A USME FVG Retest in Discount / Premium

High-probability trend-continuation setup designed for Phase 1 capital accumulation.

  1. Confirm Trend Cloud alignment — BULL or BEAR, never range.
  2. Wait for a Liquidity Sweep (BSL / SSL) during London (02:00–05:00) or NY AM (08:30–11:00 EST).
  3. Enter on the retest of a 3-candle non-repainting FVG at the 50% CE midpoint.
  4. Risk 0.5% max — TP1 at 1:1.5 R:R, TP2 at the opposite liquidity pool.
Discount + unmitigated FVG + trend cloud alignment + sweep = the continuation stack.

B Session Sweep Breaker Reversal

Reversal setup tailored for clearing Phase 2 or recovering after a minor pullback.

  1. Sweep of the prior Session High/Low during NY PM Macro (13:30–16:00 EST).
  2. Displacement breaks internal structure, creating a Breaker Block.
  3. Limit order entry on the Breaker retest with Anchored VWAP confirmation.
  4. Risk 0.25% with a strict 1:2 R:R target.
Breaker = broken order block. Old demand becomes supply. Short the retest, not the first break.

Five takeaways. Then practice.

  • Trailing drawdown locks to your equity highs — know the real floor, not the static one.
  • 0.5% risk max means 10+ consecutive losses to touch a daily limit, not two or three.
  • Phase 1 accumulate (0.5%), Phase 2 consolidate (0.25%), Funded = buffer then scale.
  • Consistency: never let a single day exceed 30–40% of the total profit target.
  • Three-confluence + circuit breakers. If one filter disagrees, you wait.

Practice on replay. Protect capital first. Education builds calm, surgical conviction — not urgency.
