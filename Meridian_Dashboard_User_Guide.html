How the alert pipeline actually works

Five steps, in order. Each step is short-circuitable — if any fails, the alert just doesn't fire. There's no retry queue.

  1. Signal prints. Backend evaluates the 3-Gate + Lattice + OFI on the latest bar. If side + grade clear the floor, the engine emits a signal frame on the WebSocket.
  2. Frontend dedupes. The dashboard keeps a per-symbol lastSignalId map. Re-broadcasts from the WS that match the last signal within cooldown are dropped.
  3. Browser notification fires. If the user enabled it and OS permission is granted, a desktop popup appears with the same one-line summary as the in-dashboard alert chip.
  4. Webhook fires. One POST to the configured URL with the payload above. Timeouts at 4s — a slow webhook doesn't queue, it drops.
  5. Journal captures. Independently of any of the above, the signal is captured to the journal (forward-only, no edits). This is why alerts and journal entries are always 1:1 — same trigger, same timestamp.
ⓘ

Honest caveats: alerts are frontend-side (the tab must be open), ~10s latency floor (WS 3s + cache + browser wakeup), not bar-by-bar like native Pine alert(). For at-the-bell execution on every signal, wire a backend-side alert dispatcher (a future capability, not in scope today). Don't point an alert at a destination that requires a guarantee — alerts are best-effort, not contractual.

How the scorecard is computed

Two numbers summarize every signal on the symbol:

Confirm %
Hit rate on resolved signals
CONFIRM ÷ (CONFIRM + BREAK). OPEN signals are excluded from the denominator. A symbol with 12 CONFIRM, 5 BREAK, 8 OPEN = 70.6%.
Profit Factor (R)
Sum R wins ÷ |Sum R losses|
≥ 1.0 means the engine is net-positive in R-terms on that symbol. Below 1.0 = the engine is paying out less than it costs. Use as a "should I keep trading this" filter.
ⓘ

Forward-only is the point. The journal will not let you delete, edit, or "re-grade" a captured signal — that's the whole honesty guarantee. If you don't like a result, your only option is to add a note explaining why, or to stop trading the symbol. The CSV/JSON export keeps the same schema, so what you see is what you ship.

8 · Engines

Under the hood.

Two engines share the dashboard. The Vol-Arb engine prices volatility — it screens options for IV vs RV mispricings and emits delta-neutral structures. The OFI engine prices direction — it reads Smart Money Concepts, VWAP, and Order Flow on top of OHLCV bars as a confirmation filter. Same dashboard, fundamentally different jobs.

Engine 1 · Volatility Arbitrage
Sell vol when IV > RV. Buy vol when IV < RV.
Options price implied vol; stocks realize vol. The mispricing between them is the edge. When IV is rich, the engine sells vol and collects premium as IV mean-reverts toward RV. When IV is cheap, it buys vol and pays up because realized vol will likely exceed what options currently price in.
Engine 2 · Order Flow Intelligence (OFI)
A confirmation filter, not a signal generator.
The engine reads OHLCV bars and derives four order-flow primitives — CVD (the running delta sum), absorption (one side pushing but price disagreeing — reversal hint), imbalance (strong delta + structural confirmation — continuation hint), and stacked imbalance (3+ consecutive bars of one-sided aggression — the rarest, most-aggressive read). Most bars print no read at all — that is the engine honestly saying "no aligned setup."

The vol-arb signal, in one block.

Strike-level IV is smile premium — tail-insurance pricing, a market structure, not mispricing. Using it to rank signals would systematically over-rank deep-OTM puts. Only ATM IV vs the matching-tenor realized-vol forecast counts.

# backend/app/engines.py — SignalEngine.compute()
edge      = round(atm_iv - rv_i, 2)          # vol points (apples-to-apples)
z         = edge / cls.SIGMA_SPREAD                  # SIGMA_SPREAD = 2.5 vol pts
side      = "SELL VOL" if edge > 0 else "BUY VOL"
confidence = "high" if |z| ≥ 1.5 else ("medium" if |z| ≥ 1.0 else "low")
edge_cost = round(|edge| × VEGA_PER_LOT - ROUND_TRIP_COST, 2)
               # VEGA_PER_LOT = $40/vol-pt · ROUND_TRIP_COST = $8/contract

A signal with |edge| < 0.01 vol points is dropped — that's "no real edge in this expiry," and the engine returns empty rather than fabricating a fill. The ranking is edge first, then ATM-first, then nearest-to-money — explicitly pushing deep-OTM put skew to the bottom of the list (it's expensive for a reason, not mispriced).

The OFI reads, as a 2 × 2 matrix.

The four OFI types are not arbitrary names — they form a clean matrix. Two axes: price-vs-delta agreement and structural confirmation.

Cell 1
Just a green bar

Cell 2 · Imbalance

strong_up + c < o + near_bear_level — buyers pushing into a bearish bar that still closed down near a bear level. Mirror of Cell 3. Same circle, opposite direction.

Why stacked imbalance is the rarest read.

A single bar of strong delta is common. A run of 3+ consecutive bars where the delta is both large (1.5× its 20-bar SMA of |delta|) and persistent (no bar in the run is allowed to fail the test) is statistically unusual. Random walks produce 3-bar runs around 12% of the time; the magnitude+persistence combo cuts that much further.

# backend/app/meridian.py — stacked imbalance (CVD proxy fallback, source="px")
depth = min(min_stack_rows + 2, 8)             # walk back 5-8 bars
for k in range(depth):
    idx = i - k
    d   = delta[idx]
    avg = avg_abs_delta[idx]
    if   avg > 0 and d >  avg * ofi_delta_mult:  run_buy  += 1; run_sell = 0
    elif avg > 0 and d < -avg * ofi_delta_mult:  run_sell += 1; run_buy  = 0
    else:                                       break   # any failed bar ends the run
if run_buy  ≥ min_stack_rows: stack_bull = True    # default min = 3
if run_sell ≥ min_stack_rows: stack_bear = True

Stacked also pushes a liquidity magnet: when it fires, the engine runs push_pool(l, 1, count × 1.5 × vol_ratio) — the swing extreme of the stacked run becomes a magnet that decays ×0.92 per bar, exactly like a sweep-pool. A self-reinforcing loop: the signal that fires hardest also creates the target TP3 will later aim at.

ⓘ

Two engines, two strategies. Vol-arb is continuous-position, multi-leg, delta-neutral, held days-to-weeks. OFI is event-driven, single bracket per signal, minutes-to-hours. What pays for vol-arb is theta + IV mean-reversion; for OFI, it's momentum or mean-reversion confirmed by structure + flow. Don't conflate them — they share a dashboard but neither shares the other's logic.

9 · Troubleshooting

When something is off.

Symptom → root cause → fix → how to prevent it. Each card is one known issue with the smallest honest path to a working dashboard. If your symptom isn't listed, GET /api/meta tells you provider / simulation / data — that's the first thing to capture.

ENGINE

"Nothing prints — every bar shows grade — and no signal."

grade: — signal: null on every bar, all symbols, all timeframes
Cause: min grade is set to SINGULAR or ELITE, or you're outside market hours on a quiet symbol. The lattice needs 2+ distinct event types within ~12 bars and ~1.5×ATR to even register WATCH.
Fix: drop min grade to WATCH in the top bar. If still nothing, try NVDA · 5m during market hours — denser bars cluster more event types. A blank dashboard on every symbol and timeframe means the engine is genuinely saying "no aligned setup anywhere" — that's the filter doing its job.
Set min grade = WATCH by default; bump up to PRIME only after you've calibrated your read on a symbol.
ENGINE

"Stacked imbalance prints on almost every bar."

stack_bull or stack_bear nearly always = true
Cause: ofi_delta_mult is too low. Default is 1.5× the 20-bar SMA of |delta|. Lower values fire the strong-delta condition too easily, so the 3-bar run threshold is met by random walk alone.
Fix: raise ofi_delta_mult to 2.0 or 2.5 in meridian.py (line 136), or pass it in the engine config. Re-test on the same symbol — stacked should become rare (a few per session, not every bar).
Default of 1.5 is calibrated for liquid 5m SPY/NQ. Raise it to 2.0+ for less-liquid symbols or higher timeframes where delta variance is naturally wider.
BROKER

"Broker badge says SIMULATED · no key — Execute is greyed out."

broker badge: SIMULATED · no key, Execute button disabled
Cause: backend/.env is missing ALPACA_API_KEY / ALPACA_SECRET_KEY, or the broker can't reach Alpaca's paper endpoint. The dashboard runs without a broker — but you can't place orders.
Fix: copy backend/.env.example → backend/.env, paste your paper keys (get them at alpaca.markets → Paper Account), restart the backend. The badge should flip to PAPER · connected.
Don't deploy without keys wired. The dashboard appears to work — it just won't execute. Always smoke-test with a fake paper trade before sharing with a user.
BROKER

"Order rejected with 409 — no signal to execute."

HTTP 409 on POST /api/broker/order
Cause: the engine state has no live signal — either the signal expired (a few bars after the trigger, by design) or you're between signals. The endpoint refuses to fabricate a fill, by design.
Fix: wait for a fresh signal (side + grade visible in the Signal & Path panel), then click Execute within the same bar window. If the signal disappeared mid-click, refresh — the WS stream resends a fresh state.
Don't poll Execute from a script faster than the 3-second WS broadcast. You'll waste calls on expired signals.
DATA

"Tape shows ○stale, ●LIVE never lights up."

tape badge: ○stale · live indicator grey
Cause: market is closed, or Alpaca's IEX endpoint isn't reachable. The tape is the live last-trade from Alpaca (IEX); it's freshest during market hours (9:30-16:00 ET).
Fix: during market hours, check that ALPACA_API_KEY is the paper key (live keys don't work for paper, paper keys work for paper). After-hours, ○stale is correct.
Don't trade on a stale tape. The engine still computes on the delayed Polygon close, so reads are honest — but executing on stale prices is the fastest way to lose money.
DATA

"LIVE · DELAYED badge never shows — always SIMULATION."

data badge: SIMULATION permanently
Cause: the engine is on the default SimulatedProvider because MERIDIAN_LIVE=1 isn't set in the backend environment, or the Polygon key is missing/invalid. The SimulatedProvider is honest — it tells you it's running on synthetic OHLCV.
Fix: set MERIDIAN_LIVE=1 and add a valid POLYGON_API_KEY to backend/.env. Restart the backend. GET /api/meta should now report simulation: false, data: "delayed".
Polygon stocks entitlement covers SPY/QQQ/IWM/NVDA/etc. Futures ES/NQ/YM/GC require a separate Polygon futures key. If the key is valid but the badge stays SIM, you're trying a non-stocks symbol.
ENGINE

"Bias is FLAT but ADX says TREND."

regime = TREND · bias = FLAT · no signals
Cause: ADX ≥ 25 (trend threshold) but the ribbon is mixed (EMA9/21/50 not aligned). TREND needs both strong ADX and an aligned ribbon. Gate 1's bias is ribbon-aware, so a mixed ribbon neutralizes bias even in a trending regime.
Fix: this is correct behavior, not a bug. Wait for the ribbon to align (one side gets all three EMAs stacked) — that takes a bar or two after a regime shift. Or switch timeframe to get a cleaner read.
Don't equate regime = TREND with a guaranteed long or short. The ribbon filter is what makes the bias chip meaningful.
UI

"Lattice shows WATC instead of WATCH."

lattice grade chip: WATC
Cause: the chip is intentionally truncated to fit the panel — WATC = WATCH. Same for PRIM, ELIT, SING.
Fix: hover the chip for the full label, or read the tooltip in the panel header.
Don't "fix" the truncated chip — it's a layout constraint, not a bug.
ENGINE

"TP2 / TP3 don't execute when the signal fills."

bracket fills TP1 only · TP2/TP3 never trigger
Cause: by design. An Alpaca bracket order is one take-profit leg + one stop-loss leg (OCO). TP2 and TP3 are journal-only scale-out targets — they show in the journal/positions view but don't have order legs attached.
Fix: to scale out, manually close partial size at TP1 and trail the rest with a stop. Or: build a custom bracket adapter that submits multiple legs (not in scope of the current broker module).
TP2/TP3 are analysis targets, not executable orders. The dashboard surfaces them so you can decide to scale — not so the engine auto-fires.
DATA

"Liquidity pools never decay — same number all session."

active_count stays at the cap (20) for hours
Cause: the magnet decay (×0.92 per bar) only kicks in once price moves away from the pool level. If price keeps revisiting the same swing high/low, the pools re-charge each reaction and never fully decay.
Fix: check top_magnet.magnet over time — if it's not decaying, the pool is the active level, not a stale one. The top magnet is what TP3 aims at; if it's stuck, you're in a consolidation.
Decay ×0.92 is per-bar, but only after price leaves. A range-bound symbol will show stable pools — that's not a bug, it's the math.
UI

"WebSocket disconnects every few minutes — state freezes."

WS goes silent · last update timestamp stale · no new grades
Cause: the backend WS broadcasts every 3 seconds, but long-lived connections through corporate proxies / VPNs sometimes idle out at 5-10 minutes. Not a bug in the engine — a network-layer timeout.
Fix: the dashboard auto-reconnects, but if it doesn't, refresh the page. The next REST snapshot re-syncs to the current bar, then WS resumes.
For long sessions, hit the page every 10 minutes or keep a tab-active script. The WS has no keepalive because the snapshot REST endpoint is the source of truth — the WS is just an overlay.
ENGINE

"Stacked imbalance fires on the same bar as absorption / imbalance."

all four OFI types true on one bar
Cause: the four predicates are independent — they're not mutually exclusive. A single bar can show absorption, imbalance, and the start of a stacked run if conditions overlap. That's the dashboard telling you a lot is happening at once.
Fix: nothing to fix — the dashboard tags stacked with source="px" in the meta so you can distinguish the CVD proxy from a real footprint (when wired).
Don't dedupe by hand. The four reads represent different things; co-firing is information, not noise.

If your symptom isn't here, capture this first: GET /api/meta (provider, simulation flag, data source, symbol, timeframe, preset, footprint capability), GET /api/health (provider + engine status), and the last 3 bars from the chart's CVD series. With those three things, the underlying issue is usually one curl away from identified.

10 · FAQ

The fine print.

Four points the rest of the guide doesn't cover in plain English. The §1 / §3 / §8 sections already explain the alert pipeline, the settings table, and the 3-gate test — this section is the things that sit underneath those, especially the conditions that prevent a signal from firing and the realistic signal frequency you should expect.

A. The three vetoes that suppress otherwise-valid signals

The 3-gate test (covered in §1 mod-gates) is necessary but not sufficient. On top of the three gates, the engine runs three additional vetoes per side. A signal can have all three gates clear and still not print, because one of these vetoes vetoed it. They're the most common "the gates were all green, why didn't it fire?" answer.

Veto 1
chase_ok
Refuses to chase. If price is already 2+ ATR extended from VWAP in the direction of the signal, the veto flips false. A long signal that fires 3 ATRs above VWAP doesn't get printed — you're not adding a position at the top of an overextended move, no matter how clean the gate logic reads. The threshold scales with the preset — 2 ATR on 5m is a different distance than 2 ATR on 4h.
Veto 2
range_fade
Refuses the wrong side of the cloud in RANGE. In a RANGE regime, the cloud is the operational boundary. A long signal below the cloud is a fade candidate, not a breakout — the veto refuses to print it. Mirrors for shorts above the cloud. The veto is silent in TREND and TRANSITION regimes where the cloud isn't the operative boundary.
Veto 3
confirmation
Requires structural confirmation. At least one of these must be true on the same bar: band touch (price tested the ±1σ / ±2σ band), FVG retest (the zone gate is satisfied, which already implies this), BOS (break of structure), or CHoCH (change of character). Without at least one, the engine refuses the signal — even if the three gates are technically all true.
ⓘ

How to read the panel. When a signal doesn't print and the gates all look green, scroll to the bottom of the gates panel and check the per-side veto indicators. Each one has a small dot: green = vetoed false (OK to print), amber = vetoed true (the side is suppressed). The dashboard surfaces all six states (3 vetoes × 2 sides) on every bar. If a side is "all green" and still no signal, the issue is downstream — chase_ok or the grade filter, not the gate.

B. The tiebreaker — when both sides gate, the engine doesn't commit

It's possible for the engine to compute buy_raw = true AND sell_raw = true on the same bar. This happens in tight ranges where ribbon bias is mixed, both sides have FVG zones near price, and the OFI read is ambiguous. When both raw signals are true, the engine doesn't print either — it would be guessing.

The full tiebreaker rule, in priority order:

  1. One side is true, the other is false. The true side prints. Standard case — most signals come through this path.
  2. Both sides are true, lattice grades differ. The side with the higher lattice grade wins. PRIME+ on the long side beats WATCH on the short side, even if both raw signals fired. The dashboard's "winning" side will be the one with the higher grade — and the tiebreaker is silent: a PRIME+ long with WATCH short just looks like a normal PRIME+ long.
  3. Both sides are true, lattice grades are equal. No signal prints. The engine refuses to commit to a direction it can't distinguish. This is the rare-but-important case — a "phantom" bar where the setup looked ready on both sides but the engine didn't fire. It's not a bug; it's the filter saying "I can't tell which one is real right now."
ⓘ

What to do on a phantom bar. Wait. The next bar (or two) will usually resolve the ambiguity one way or the other as the OFI read firms up. If you force a trade on a phantom bar, you're betting against the engine's tiebreaker — sometimes right, often wrong. The phantom is information: the market hasn't picked a side yet, and the trade isn't there.

C. The armed window scales with your timeframe

When a trigger fires (sweep, VWAP reclaim, or CHoCH), the engine marks the bar as bull_armed or bear_armed. The "armed" state expires after a number of bars — the armed window. A long signal has to print while the side is still armed, otherwise the gate fails on the timing alone.

The armed window is not a constant. It's automatically derived from the active preset:

# meridian.py:146 — auto-derives from the preset's sweep length
armed_bars = max(3, self.sweep_len // 2)

# preset -> sweep_len -> armed window
Scalp:    sweep_len=6   →  armed window = 3 bars   (5m, 15m)
Intraday: sweep_len=10  →  armed window = 5 bars   (5m, 15m, 30m)
Swing:    sweep_len=12  →  armed window = 6 bars   (1h, 4h)
Position: sweep_len=15  →  armed window = 7 bars   (1d)

Why this matters: on a 5m Scalp preset, a trigger fires and the engine has 3 bars to confirm the signal before the armed state expires. On a 1d Position preset, you have 7 bars — which on daily is 7 trading days. A trigger that fires on Monday has until the following Monday to be confirmed. Same trigger, radically different windows.

The max(3, ...) floor exists so even the shortest preset gives the signal a real chance to set up. A 1-bar armed window would mean "trigger and confirm on the same bar" — which collapses the gate into noise. Three bars is the minimum the engine considers a valid setup window.

Why the asymmetry between BUY and SELL

In a typical US session, you'll see roughly 1.5-2× more BUY signals than SELL signals on 5m SPY/NVDA charts. The asymmetry comes from two compounding factors:

  • Default skew in the OFI source. The CVD proxy biases toward aggressive buyers because flat bars split volume 50/50 to up_vol, and the equity market trends to have more flat-to-up bars than flat-to-down over time.
  • Longer hold times favor continuations. BUY signals that fire on a strong bar tend to run further than SELL signals at the same grade, because the structural support below is denser (more resting bids, more visible stops on the other side). The engine's TP2/TP3 levels reflect this implicitly — TP2 is priced higher in TREND.

If you're trading both sides and the ratio is significantly off (say, 4-5× more of one side), check the regime chip. TRANSITION regime is structurally harder for the engine to commit to a side; most phantom bars (the tiebreaker case above) cluster in TRANSITION, and the engine will print fewer of either side as a result.

ⓘ

Don't lower the bar to get more signals. The grade filter is the dial. If you want more signals, drop to WATCH and expect roughly 3-5× more prints — but at the cost of grade quality. The Confirm % on the journal will reflect this: WATCH-grade signals have a lower hit rate than PRIME+. If your Confirm % drops after lowering the floor, that's the data telling you the lower-grade signals weren't edge. Raise the floor again.
